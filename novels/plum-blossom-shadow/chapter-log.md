## Chapter 1: The Honest Blade
- **Words:** 3,646
- **Summary:** In Elder Fang's Socratic theory class for Body Tempering students (22 present), Meiying notices his left sleeve is shorter than his right and deduces a hurried morning and an outside letter. She helps Chengqi (anchoring at the shoulder instead of the elbow), Suyin and a Binding Sash girl complete the Qi cycle, and answers Elder Fang's intent question correctly. Wen Jiabao of the Flame Herald branch calls her a genius whose path was "handed" to her; she sets terms of no weapons, Qi and body only, first to yield, and ends it with a single chest strike (Zhǐ Huā) as he fully infuses his fists, with Elder Fang calling a halt. Afterwards she tells Wen Jiabao exactly what he did well and that he leads with his right shoulder before every power strike. An Iron Sash senior disciple with a sealed letter case passes them heading back toward Elder Fang's classroom, and they arrive at the newly moved-up Burden class.
- **New facts:** Elder Fang (theory instructor; Socratic; praises no one: says "Correct" and moves on; cut his own left sleeve short that morning). Inscription above the Still Blade training hall. Class content: offensive Qi moves forward and compresses, defensive Qi spreads and anchors, projection Qi releases; practical exercise: full cycle with compression on the forward pass and release on the return, hold ten seconds. Meiying has worked with Qi since age nine; her warm Qi is a Ten Legendary Swords trait; her third meridian point has resisted since age twelve (three attempts, workaround only). Wen Jiabao (Flame Herald, broad, Qi-infused punches, orange heat on his fists). Luo Chengqi (draws a three-winged bird). Suyin (deadpan). The Iron Vow boy (unnamed in Ch1; watches where Meiying's eyes go; leaves without a word). The Binding Sash girl (third row, grips the brush hard). The Iron Sash senior disciple courier (about 19 or 20). Burden class "Applied Theory of Burdens and Conditional Qi — Introduction", moved to this term from third year. Meiying's hand runs the Honest Blade guard when thinking.
- **Power / stat changes:** Zhǐ Huā used in combat (Mastered, per compendium). Took one Qi-enhanced rib hit and one leg kick; ribs will bruise. No realm change.
- **Threads opened:** Elder Fang's letter and the courier; the Iron Vow boy; Wen Jiabao's reaction; the third meridian resistance; why the Burden class was moved; Suyin's remark that Wen Jiabao will think about what she told him, not about losing.
- **Threads closed:** None.
- **Ending hook:** Meiying walks to the Burden class "looking forward" to a long day, thinking of the third meridian, the courier, and whether Burden theory matches what she already suspects.

## Chapter 2: The Weight of Want
- **Words:** 2,623
- **Summary:** In Elder Shen Cailan's circular-desk Burden class, mixed branches and realms, four students raise their hands as never taking a Burden and Shen Cailan tells them to hold onto that. She defines a Burden as a contract, lists three types (External, Internal, Existential), and the advantages (faster Qi absorption, higher breakthrough success, a higher ceiling) and costs (every Burden has a condition; broken conditions produce backlash; cultivators have died). Suyin, normally nearly silent, argues that External Burdens are easier to lose rather than easier to carry, and Shen Cailan concedes the point. Afterwards Suyin tells Meiying she has had Burden notes for three years, since her sister took one without enough information, and Chengqi announces he wants a Burden. Meiying privately asks herself what she would carry one for and has no answer.
- **New facts:** Elder Shen Cailan (fifties, unhurried, uses the students' scepticism). Burden rules from lecture: External = person, place or cause; Internal = limit or seal yourself; Existential = bound to fate (curse, shared destiny), rare. Burdens do not replace strength; they make existing strength more expensive and more valuable. Burdens taken before Foundation Establishment integrate more deeply (higher amplification and risk); later ones are more stable but less transformative. Backlash severity is proportional to integration depth. Maintenance cost is the amplification; the breaking cost is the backlash. Compatibility depends on path, constellation and what the cultivator "actually is". Suyin's older sister took a Burden three years ago (author's note: older sister, unnamed). Suyin has known Meiying two years. Four hands raised; Meiying did not raise hers. Family philosophy question from a quiet unrecognised Iron Vow student (Meiying heard the answer "land somewhere she did not examine"). Chengqi: "I want one."
- **Power / stat changes:** None.
- **Threads opened:** Suyin's sister and her Burden; why Burden theory is the only subject that makes Suyin talkative; Shen Cailan's refusal to answer what a catastrophic Burden break at Core Formation or above does to constellation progress; Meiying's "what would I carry one for" question; Chengqi's wish for a Burden; the quiet Iron Vow student (unidentified).
- **Threads closed:** None. (Burden theory is introduced; whether it matches Meiying's private suspicion from Ch1 is not yet resolved.)
- **Ending hook:** Walking to the training courts, Meiying realises the question "what would I carry one for" now matters, and does not finish the thought.

Wanru: senior disciple width storm-blue trim, fights fluidly, "dissolves" Meiying's read. Meiying's jian fitted at age ten.
