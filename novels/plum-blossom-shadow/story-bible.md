# Story Bible: Plum Blossom Shadow

This file is the source of truth for the novel. Read it before every chapter and never contradict it.

**Where this comes from.** This bible was rebuilt from the author's own material: chapters 1 to 6, the four-volume world compendium, and the author's end-of-chapter notes. Nothing from the earlier invented plan has been kept.

**The author's delegation.** After reading the open questions, the author handed every remaining decision to the lead writer: *"Let the lead writer choose its own answers. I'm giving it full control of the story."* Every answer the lead writer chose under that delegation is now canon and is marked **(LW)**. A one-line list of each decision is in **Decided by the lead writer** at the end, so the author can see what was chosen and overrule anything.

**Which source wins.** When sources disagree, the newest one wins:
1. Lead-writer decisions (LW), made with the author's delegation
2. The author's chapters and chapter notes written after Compendium Volume IV (chapters 2 to 6 and their notes)
3. Compendium Volume IV (World Foundation)
4. Volume III (Technique Systems)
5. Volume II
6. Volume I

**Source markers.** (Ch 3) = chapter 3. (Vol II) = compendium Volume II. (Notes 5) = the author's notes after chapter 5. (LW) = decided by the lead writer.

**What the reader knows vs what the writers know.** Much of this bible is hidden from the reader and from Meiying. Sections marked **Hidden** must be revealed only on the schedule in **Reveal schedule**, and only through Meiying's point of view.

## The basics

- **Title:** Plum Blossom Shadow (梅影, Méiyǐng). The title works on three levels, all of them hidden from the reader at the start and clear by the end (Vol IV):
  - **A name:** it is the protagonist's name. 梅 (méi) = plum blossom, 影 (yǐng) = shadow. The reader meets her on page one without knowing the title is her name.
  - **A destination:** the Plum Blossom Shadow is the tenth and final aspect of the Constellation of the Ten Legendary Swords. No sect manual describes it. It is what she is becoming before she knows what it is.
  - **A theme:** her journey asks what it means to be a cultivator. The answer, if there is one, is inside the thing the title names.
- **Genre(s):** Cultivation / Murim / progression fantasy (Vol IV). At its core, a cultivation mystery and adventure: three young disciples uncover a conspiracy inside their sect while the heroine grows into a powerful cultivator (author's premise).
- **Logline (LW, final wording):** When the Sect Head's youngest daughter and her two friends witness a secret meeting no one was meant to see, their teacher dies of a broken vow, and the only way to find out who broke it is to climb faster, fight harder and see further than anyone expects of a girl walking her sect's most demanding sword path.
- **Target platform (LW):** Royal Road first. Build a backlog of 10 to 15 chapters, then release 3 chapters a week. Kindle Unlimited later, as revised books.
- **Target chapter length (LW):** 2,500 to 3,500 words. Never over 4,000. (The author's chapters run 2,200 to 4,400; Royal Road readers favour the lower end.)
- **POV and tense:** Close third person, past tense, Chu Meiying's point of view only. All six chapters keep strictly to her POV.
- **Tone:** Serious and epic (author). The world is "vast and ancient without being dark. Scale that serves the journey rather than threatens it" (Vol IV). Dry, character-driven humour runs through every chapter (Meiying's observations, Chengqi's enthusiasm, Suyin's deadpan) without undercutting the stakes.
- **Content limits (LW):**
  - Violence: on the page, grounded and consequential. Injuries hurt and last. Deaths matter. Blood is allowed; lingering gore and torture scenes are not.
  - Sexual content: none.
  - Swearing: rare and in-world (oaths and insults that belong to the setting). No modern profanity.
  - **Romance: none planned.** Meiying is 14. Friendship and family are the emotional core. If a romance is ever considered, it is for an adult Meiying after a time skip, and only if the author asks for it.
- **Comparable novels (LW):** *Forge of Destiny* (female lead, sect life, slow-rising threat), *Cradle* (clear, satisfying progression), *Mother of Learning* (planted clues that pay off later), *A Thousand Li* and *Cultivation Nerd* (grounded sect cultivation, cleverness as power). Tone references: *Nirvana in Fire* (patient political mystery) and *Grandmaster of Demonic Cultivation* (a mystery spine inside sect politics).

## Pacing and growth rules (standing direction from the author)

The author's direction, word for word: *"My only suggestion is it says slow mystery might lose readers. Since typically an fantasy like this depends on action and protagonist development it can increase those actions."*

This is a standing rule for the whole team, every arc. The mystery stays a slow burn in what it **reveals**, never in what **happens**.

1. **Action on the page, often.** At least every second chapter has an action set-piece: a fight, a spar with consequences, a chase, a beast encounter on the slopes, a night search or break-in, a test under pressure. Never more than two chapters in a row without one.
2. **Visible growth in every chapter.** Every chapter moves Meiying at least one step on her progression ladder (below), even a small one, and names it plainly in the prose so readers can track it. Every 3 to 5 chapters brings a bigger step: a form rising a level, a realm stage, a new sense, an aspect.
3. **The mystery advances through action.** Clues are found by doing: following, searching, sensing, fighting, getting caught. Being told things in rooms is the exception. Every investigation chapter pairs a clue with a progression beat.
4. **Talk scenes are short and carry conflict.** Characters want different things in every conversation. World information arrives when Meiying needs it to make a choice.
5. **Growth is earned and costs something:** hours, injuries, risk, lost time, a friend's piece of the answer ("neither piece alone would have been enough", Ch 4). Never "genius".
6. **Stakes keep pace with her.** Each step up meets a stronger test: Han Jingwei, Qi Gathering seniors, her own siblings, the watcher, people who announce nothing. Some costs cannot be fought (politics, family, secrets).
7. **Her friends grow too.** Suyin and Chengqi get their own visible progress, slower than hers, so the trio stays a trio.
8. **Tone holds.** Serious and epic. Humour comes from character, never from undercutting a fight or a death.
9. **Pace targets for Arc 1:** the breakthrough to Qi Gathering lands no later than chapter 10. In early arcs, one realm stage roughly every 10 to 15 chapters, with form and skill steps between them.

### Meiying's progression ladder (what can move)
Track all of these in the chapter log, in words and plain numbers. Never as LitRPG screens.
- **Realm and stage** (Body Tempering Peak now).
- **Aspects unlocked** (1 of 10 now).
- **Form mastery** for each of the seven forms of each aspect (Not Yet, Can Perform, Reliable, Mastered).
- **Endurance in sequences:** how many full technique sequences she can run before her Qi thins. Through the secondary channel: 3 now (the forward pass: 5 or 6). From Qi Gathering, sequences also measure her Qi reservoir.
- **Qi sense range** in paces (from Qi Gathering).
- **Qi expression skills** from Qi Gathering: blade infusion, the Qi edge, the Qi step, holding Qi under the surface during the Honest Blade (restraint).
- **Perception milestones** (her unusual sense; see Power system).
- **Burdens** carried.

## Premise

### The author's premise

> Chu Meiying is a young disciple of the Jianyun Sect whose unusually sharp perception draws her into a mystery hidden beneath ordinary sect life. While training, cultivating, and spending time with her friends Suyin and Luo Chengqi, the three accidentally witness a secret meeting between a Jianyun elder and a mysterious outsider, something they were never meant to see. Their discovery coincides with increasingly suspicious events inside the sect, including Elder Fang's mysterious death, a sealed letter, an unknown geometric emblem, and indications that members of the sect may be involved in something much larger. As Meiying and her friends quietly investigate, they risk attracting the attention of people far more powerful than themselves. At the same time, Meiying is progressing through her own cultivation journey, discovering that her ability to perceive and understand Qi may be far more unusual than she initially realizes.
>
> At its core: a cultivation mystery/adventure about three young disciples uncovering a conspiracy within their sect while the heroine gradually grows from an ordinary disciple into a powerful cultivator.

"Ordinary disciple" means her starting realm (Body Tempering), not her status: she is the Sect Head's daughter (LW).

### The novel as the author has built it

Chu Meiying, 14, is the youngest of five children of Chu Wulong, Sect Head of the Jianyun Sect. The sect has practised the Path of the Eight Guardian Constellations for over six centuries. Every disciple discovers their constellation during Body Tempering; it cannot be chosen. Meiying was expected to awaken to something soft and appropriate. Instead she awakened to the Ten Legendary Swords, the most technically demanding constellation in the path, which has not produced a true master in two generations. The sect called it genius. She went home and trained for six more hours.

The story opens with her at the peak of Body Tempering, weeks from her first breakthrough. Within a few days she notices Elder Fang's hastily cut sleeve and a sealed letter arriving by Iron Sash courier, beats a proud Flame Herald boy in a classroom spar, and starts a class on Burdens (optional, binding contracts that amplify cultivation at a cost). On an herb run, she, Suyin and Chengqi see a Shifting Wind elder meeting a foreign figure inside a field of total silence; the outsider wears a geometric emblem that Meiying later traces to the isolated eastern highlands, with no formal contact in 112 years. Days later Elder Fang is found dead: Qi implosion, consistent with a broken Burden nobody knew he carried. Meanwhile the political fault lines of an eight-branch sect with no named heir are already shifting around her.

**Theme:** What it means to be a cultivator (Vol IV). Running underneath the chapters: whether strength comes only from the self (her father's philosophy) or from what you are willing to carry and who you let help you (Ch 2, Ch 4).

### The truth under the mystery (Hidden; LW)
The full answer, for the writers. Details are in **The Accord of the Closed Figure** and **Reveal schedule**.
- 112 years ago the sect made a secret pact with the eastern highland houses of **Qiongling**: the **Accord of the Closed Figure**. Its sign is a two-part stone seal. The highlanders keep the closed half (the emblem Meiying saw); the sect keeps the open half.
- **Elder Fang was the custodian** of the sect's half, bound by a secret Burden to keep it inside the mountain's boundary stones.
- **The Shifting Wind elder in the hollow is Elder Mo Qiyuan**, the sect's secret envoy to Qiongling, officially away on a long contract for six years. The outsider is **Hesu**, Qiongling's envoy, who came early to warn him that a breakaway highland house is moving against the Accord.
- **Fang was murdered through the rules.** Someone took the sect's half from where Fang had hidden it and carried it past the boundary stones. The condition broke; the backlash killed him. The letter did not kill him; Meiying's "immediate backlash" theory (Ch 5) is wrong.
- **The second hand is Shen Wuque**, who owes a Burden to the breakaway house. An Iron Sash posting seal he used came from **Chu Lifen**, Meiying's sister, who lent it without knowing what it was for.

## Protagonist

### Chu Meiying (楚梅影)

- **Name:** Chu Meiying. Meaning: "Plum Blossom Shadow". Servants and archive staff call her "my lady" (Ch 4). Han Jingwei calls her "the Sect Head's daughter" (Ch 6).
- **Age:** 14 at story start (Vol II).
- **Appearance:** The author's only note: "She is beautiful in a way she genuinely does not think about. It is other people's problem, not hers" (Vol II). Writers may add small concrete details when the scene needs them (a bruise, ink on her fingers, hair tied back for training), but keep her looks out of focus: she does not think about them, so the narration does not either.
- **Position:** Youngest daughter of the Sect Head. Lives in the Chu family residence (Ch 4, Ch 5).
- **Path / constellation / branch:** Path of the Eight Guardian Constellations; Constellation of the Ten Legendary Swords; Still Blade branch (white trim).
- **Life before the story:**
  - Has worked with Qi since age 9; has gathered herbs on the outer slopes since 9; has studied political scrolls in the family archive since 9 (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 4).
  - Her jian was fitted to her reach at age 10; the grip is worn to her hand. It is shorter than standard (Ch 4, Ch 6).
  - The third meridian resistance has been with her since age 12 (Ch 1).
  - Awakened to the Ten Legendary Swords "on her first try" (Wen Jiahao, Ch 1), at 13 (LW).
  - All Chu children study politics as a compulsory family curriculum: diplomatic history, territorial records, reading what is happening beneath what is said. Her father's reasoning, said once: a sect head's family that does not understand the world is a liability (Ch 4).
  - She has known Suyin for two years (Ch 2).
- **Personality (Vol II, shown in the chapters):**
  - Talks constantly, out of genuine engagement, not nerves. Finds people and problems interesting and wants to discuss everything immediately.
  - Funny in a way that catches people off guard: sharp observations delivered casually, then a smile as if she said nothing unusual.
  - Notices everything: sleeves, posture, where people's attention goes, who grips a brush too hard. She "files" observations away and returns to them (Ch 1 to 6).
  - Helps quietly: corrects Chengqi's anchoring, touches Suyin's desk at the catch point, passes a folded note to a struggling Binding Sash girl (Ch 1).
  - Tells people the exact truth about what they did wrong and right, without thinking about what that truth will do to them (Ch 1; Suyin points this out).
  - **Hates being called a genius.** Not out of humility: the word erases the hours she bled for every result. She does not get loud. She goes quiet, which is worse. Her father is the only one allowed to say it (Vol I, Vol II).
- **What she wants:**
  - To master her constellation. She trains past midnight, three hours past lights out (Vol II).
  - To understand things completely: "I'm going to think about it until I know what it is" (Ch 1).
  - Since Chapter 5: to find out what connects the meeting, the letter and Elder Fang's death.
- **What she needs:** To accept that strength is not purely self-made. Her breakthrough in Ch 4 came from Wanru's offhand words plus a scroll she had dismissed: "Neither piece alone would have been enough." The Burden question, "What would she carry one for?", is the outline of something she does not yet have words for (Ch 2). Her first answer is her friends (LW; Arc 1).
- **Flaws shown on the page:** Delivers truth without managing its effect (Ch 1). Pushes a little further than agreed ("Not far," Ch 3), which is how the trio ended up at the hollow. Her honesty makes keeping secrets cost her: the Honest Blade's creed is "nothing added, nothing withheld", and the investigation will force her to withhold (LW). Her mother's flaw is protecting her from truths, so Meiying does not yet know what is being kept from her (Vol II).
- **Starting cultivation (Ch 1; Vol IV):** Body Tempering, Peak; breakthrough to Qi Gathering imminent. One aspect unlocked of ten (the Honest Blade). No Burdens. Full status is under **Meiying's progression** below.
- **Her Qi:** Runs warm, a known characteristic of the Ten Legendary Swords. It means she must be more precise than other students about where she directs it, and it makes Rooted Shore harder to calibrate (Ch 1, Vol III).
- **Her perception (LW):** On the page so far it is reading bodies, habits and visible or felt Qi (Ch 1, Ch 6). What makes it "far more unusual than she realizes" is that she reads how Qi is **built**, not just where it is. It grows out of the Honest Blade and her constellation, step by step. See **Meiying's perception** under Power system.
- **Way of speaking:**
  - Fast, precise, declarative. Corrects people without stopping the conversation.
  - Very short answers when she is certain: "Yes." "Hit him." "Do they."
  - Wry reframes: "We're not supposed to go without awareness of the risks. Which is different."
  - Teasing deflection: "I'm not walking fast. You're walking slow."
  - Never announces feelings. Shows them by going quiet, by setting something down slowly, by changing the terms of a challenge.

## Main characters

### Wei Suyin

- **Role:** Meiying's close friend. One of the investigating trio.
- **Age / branch / constellation / realm:** 14; Iron Vow branch (deep crimson trim); Crescent Blade; Body Tempering, Mid stage (Vol III, Vol IV, Ch 3).
- **Personality:** Quiet, observant, deadpan. Says one thing every twenty minutes, and it is always the most accurate thing said all day (Vol III). The corner of her mouth moving is her version of a full laugh (Ch 1). Silence is a tool, not emptiness; she is "selective" (Ch 2).
- **Hidden depth:** Burden theory is the one subject that makes her talkative. She has three years of dense notes on Burdens and corrected Elder Shen Cailan in class (Ch 2). She collects old Burden-contract texts (LW).
- **Her sister (LW):** **Wei Jinglan**, 20, Iron Vow senior disciple, Crescent Blade, Qi Gathering Mid. Three years ago, at 17, she took an External Burden binding her to hold the Ninth Ford garrison on a long Iron Vow contract, "without enough information". The early months were hard; she is "managing it well" now. She is posted far from the mountain, so she stays offstage in Arc 1 (letters only) and can appear in Arc 2 or 3.
- **Habits:** Carries a gathering satchel and a damp cloth for herbs; knows herbs well; keeps a small personal notebook and catalogues things quickly (the Bond cultivator, Ch 3). Her room in the Iron Vow residential building is small and exactly organised: books on every surface in an order that looks random and is not, a low desk with three ink stones, one east window (Ch 5).
- **In the investigation:** Thinks she has seen the emblem somewhere in her reading. By Ch 6 she has "found something" and will know "tonight" if it is the right thing (Ch 3, Ch 6). What she found (LW): an old Burden-contract text in her own collection describing "closed-figure" binding seals from the east, which means the outsider's emblem is **a binding seal, not a crest**. She also saw the angle Meiying missed: if Fang's Burden was not in the Iron Sash records, someone helped him hide it; if it was, the Iron Sash already knows what broke (Ch 5). (The answer: it was in the records.)
- **Current state (Ch 6):** Distracted enough to lose a spar to Luo Yanzi. Meiying knows why and says nothing.
- **What she wants:** Not to let anyone she loves walk into something without enough light (Ch 2).
- **Growth (LW):** Her investigation role is Burden logic. Her progression role is Crescent Blade endurance; she reaches Body Tempering Late during Arc 1.
- **Way of speaking:** Short, exact, unadorned. "So?" "I implied it." "Accurate and uninformative." Precision rather than reassurance: "We don't have enough information to know what it looks like yet."

### Luo Chengqi

- **Role:** Meiying's close friend. One of the investigating trio.
- **Age / branch / constellation / realm:** 14; Shifting Wind branch (storm-blue trim); Infinite Staff; Body Tempering, Mid stage (Vol III, Ch 3). Fights with a practice staff (Ch 6).
- **Personality:** Enthusiastic, easily distracted, genuinely kind. Interested in twelve things at once. Laughs easily and means it every time. Gets details slightly wrong; Meiying corrects him mid-sentence (Vol III). Wants things fully first and works backward to understanding: "emotionally ahead of himself by exactly one step" (Ch 2). Draws birds with three wings ("It's more stable that way," Ch 1). Has large hands that can be surprisingly delicate (Ch 3). Has loved one stall's rice cakes with dark sauce since he was eleven (Ch 3).
- **Fighting:** Unorthodox. He finds an opponent's rhythm, declines to be where they expect, and waits until they compensate for where he is not going to be. He beat a heavier Iron Vow student this way (Ch 6).
- **Burden:** "I've decided that I want a Burden." He does not know which kind or why (Ch 2). He takes his first Burden in Arc 2, and it costs him (LW).
- **Branch loyalty (key thread):** Seeing a Shifting Wind elder at the secret meeting took the comedy out of him (Ch 3). By Ch 5 he has a name he will not say yet: "Once I say it I can't unsay it." This is the most careful restraint he has shown (Notes 5). The name (LW) is **Elder Mo Qiyuan**: the only senior Shifting Wind elder of that age Chengqi has never met, because the roll says Mo has been away on a long contract for six years. His loyalty to his branch and to what is right comes into tension when asking questions inside his branch gets him warned off (Notes 3, LW).
- **Growth (LW):** His investigation role is movement, routes and his branch's insides. His progression role is unpredictability; he reaches Body Tempering Late during Arc 1.
- **Way of speaking:** Runs ahead of his own sentences ("Okay so that was — the end, how did you —"). Repeats himself when determined ("I know I want one"). Literal questions ("To who?"). When serious, slow and careful, "like he was working out the grammar of something in a language he was not fluent in" (Ch 3).

### The trio together

Meiying fills silences; Suyin uses silence as a tool; Chengqi responds to everything (Vol III). They walk together between classes without deciding to (Ch 2). After the meeting they agreed: tell no one until they know what it means, because "we don't know whose side we're on yet" (Ch 3). After Fang's death Meiying set one day for more information, then they will tell someone, probably Rou Shenglan (Ch 5).

## The Chu family

The sect's founding bloodline and its most visible fault line: a distant, formal father who loves like a mountain; a warm mother who quietly architects the household's emotional and political reality; four older siblings living inside the silence of an unnamed succession; and Meiying at the bottom of the birth order (Vol II).

### Chu Wulong (楚武龙, "Martial Dragon"), the father
- 52. Sect Head. Head of the Chu House branch. Celestial Sword. Core Formation, Late stage.
- Became Sect Head at 31 when his own father died mid-breakthrough. He was not ready, decided he could never be unready again, and that decision hardened into who he is.
- Not cruel. Absolute composure, often mistaken for coldness. He sees his children clearly (he knows Meiying trains past lights out; he knows which son lies to him) but cannot close the distance between what he feels and what he shows.
- Watches Meiying more carefully than any of the others and has never told her why. **He has not told her everything about her constellation.** He is the only person allowed to call her a genius.
- Philosophy Meiying grew up inside: true strength comes from the self; any measure that needs outside contribution is incomplete (Ch 4).
- His working chambers are the administrative centre of the family's sect authority (Ch 5). His presence settles a room before he speaks (Celestial Sword suppression).
- **What he knows (Hidden; LW):**
  - He knows the Accord of the Closed Figure exists; every Sect Head is told on taking office. He and Rou Shenglan chose Elder Fang as custodian together.
  - The Iron Sash disciples Meiying watched go into his chambers in Ch 5 were reporting Fang's death to him. From that morning he knows the sect's half of the seal is missing. He and Rou are hunting it quietly.
  - He does **not** know his daughter saw the meeting in the hollow, or that Lifen's seal was used.
- **Why he named her Meiying, and what he has not told her (Hidden; LW):** On becoming Sect Head he read Chu Linfeng's sealed record, kept in the Sect Head's own archive, which names the tenth aspect of the Ten Legendary Swords: the Plum Blossom Shadow. Seven years later he gave the name to his fifth child as a private hope he has never confessed. When she actually awakened to the Ten Legendary Swords, the hope turned into fear. The same record says Chu Linfeng did not believe the last aspects could be reached by someone who carries nothing, and Chu Linfeng died at 41 when a Burden broke. Her father's "strength comes from the self" is partly an attempt to keep her away from that cost. Her mother knows why she was named; Meiying does not.

### Chu Yanmei (楚燕梅, "Swallow Blossom"), the mother
- 48. Sect Head's wife and the unofficial political anchor of the sect. Quenching Fan; pale jade trim. Foundation Establishment, Peak.
- Open, warm, physically present; remembers every birthday. Grew up in a political family and chose this marriage knowingly. She does not manipulate; she architects: what reaches her husband and when, which alliances are actually stable.
- Closest to Meiying; Meiying is her favourite (never said aloud). After the genius comments she held Meiying and said: "Then make them watch."
- Flaw: protects her children from truths they may need. She knows why Meiying was named and has chosen not to tell her (LW).
- Close professional relationship with Pei Lingzhu (same constellation) for two decades.
- Seen leaving the upper colonnade after Wanru's spar in Ch 4, without stopping. She saw the spar and chose not to ask (LW).
- She does not know about the Accord (LW).

### The succession problem
Chu Wulong has not named an heir. He is not dying, but the silence has lasted long enough that Tianlong is anxious, Lifen is positioning, Bairen is performing indifference and Wanru is waiting. None of them have considered that Meiying might matter to the question. She has not thought about succession at all, which may be exactly why her father watches her (Vol II).

If Chu Wulong dies or steps down without naming a successor, the position does **not** pass automatically to his children: any branch head strong enough may challenge for it. The Chu family currently holds a narrow majority of loyal branches. The Chu family has held the Sect Head position for four generations; this is not guaranteed to continue (Vol II). See **Sect history** for how the Chu lost and regained it (LW).

## The Jianyun Sect: people

### Teachers and staff

**Elder Fang** (deceased). Full name **Fang Shouyi** (方守一, "Keeps the One") (LW).
- Theory and technique instructor for Body Tempering students. Appears mid to late fifties; 57 (LW). Student nickname: "Elder Fang Ruins" (Vol III).
- **Branch and realm (LW):** Iron Vow by constellation (Crescent Blade), Foundation Establishment, Late. As an **instruction elder** he gave up his branch council seat (see Robes and rank), which is why Chengqi says he "wasn't a branch elder" (Ch 5).
- Socratic. Creates space rather than filling it. Does not praise; says "Correct" and moves on. Infinite patience for students who struggle while trying, none for those who do not try. Treats Meiying exactly like everyone else, which she considers his highest compliment (Ch 1, Vol III, Vol IV).
- Not warm. Correct, professional, and occasionally said things that mattered without acknowledging it (Ch 5).

## World

### Wàntiān (万天, "Ten Thousand Heavens")
- The world. World-scale and ancient beyond any living sect's memory. Cultivation has existed so long it has become political: simply what humans do when they reach far enough. Most people inside the system have stopped asking why it is the way it is (Vol IV).
- Politically active and mercantile at the civilised centre; frontier and unmapped at the edges. What lies beyond the territories the sect knows is **deliberately unmapped** for now.
- **The eastern highlands (Qiongling, 穹岭, "Vault Ridges"; LW):** a region two months' travel away, politically isolated, quiet for over a century. Archive traces (Ch 4): a trade delegation record from 40 years ago mentioning geometric insignia in an eastern highland style; a territorial note from 80 years ago with a margin drawing matching the emblem's visual language; a diplomatic record from 112 years ago, the last formal contact, three lines long: a meeting requested and never granted. The hollow meeting looks like the first contact in over a century. **(Hidden; LW)** The 112-year record is a deliberate false filing. The 80-year margin drawing is a sketch of the sect's own half of the seal (the open figure).
- **The Qiongling houses (Hidden; LW):** ruled by a council of old houses. Their cultivators walk the **Path of the Hidden** in a seal-craft tradition: they bind agreements, spaces and promises with Burdens, and their emblems are binding seals, not crests. One breakaway house wants the Accord broken; its reasons belong to Arc 3.
- Other sects exist and visit (the Verdant Ring's Bond cultivator, Ch 3). Rival sects and wider politics are developed in Arc 2 and 3.

### The Accord of the Closed Figure (Hidden; LW)
The secret at the centre of Arc 1.
- **Origin:** 112 years ago Qiongling asked for a meeting. It was granted in secret, at the hollow on the north slope, and the Accord was sealed there. The archive says "never granted" on purpose.
- **Terms:**
  1. Mutual non-interference and secrecy. Neither side names the other in public dealings.
  2. **The One Call:** whoever bears the sect's half at a renewal may, once, call on the Qiongling houses for aid, and the houses must answer. In 112 years the sect has never spent it. It is the most valuable political weapon in the sect, and almost no one knows it exists.
  3. **Renewal every seven years** at a new moon (the coming one is the sixteenth renewal), in the hollow, by the two envoys speaking inside a sealed hearing (the silence domain) with the two halves of the seal briefly joined. If the halves are not joined at a due renewal, the Accord lapses.
  4. What the sect gave in return 112 years ago is left for Arc 3.
- **The seal:** a disc of dark, dense stone, split in two and carved with interlocking angular lines. The highlanders keep the **closed figure** (the emblem Hesu wore at his collar as its sign). The sect keeps the **open figure**. When the two halves come within a few li of each other, both stir: they warm and seep a dark binding ink. That is why Fang's sleeve was stained and cut on the morning Hesu came near the mountain (Ch 1).
- **The custodian's Burden:** External (custodial). Condition: **the sect's half must not leave the mountain's boundary stones while the custodian lives.** Amplification: the custodian's steady rise (Fang reached Foundation Establishment, Late). Penalty: load-bearing backlash. The custodian is chosen by the Sect Head and the Iron Sash head together; the Burden is recorded under a sealed entry in the Iron Sash records that only the Iron Sash head may open. The custodian traditionally holds the Body Tempering theory post, so **whoever fills Fang's post is meant to be the next custodian**.
- **The boundary stones:** old, waist-high marker stones carved with the founding script, set in a ring around the mountain well outside the outer walls, on the slopes and in the cloud line. Outer disciples leave offerings at the old gate-shrines near them. Ordinary people treat them as superstition.
- **The five who know the whole Accord:** the Sect Head, the Iron Sash head, the Shifting Wind head, the custodian, the envoy. Fang Ruochen knows part of it.
- **The current state at the end of Ch 6:** the sect's half is in Shen Wuque's hands, off the mountain or hidden near the boundary. The next renewal is due at the coming new moon. Hesu came about three weeks early to warn Mo that the breakaway house was moving.

### Sect history (LW)
- The Jianyun Sect was founded over six centuries ago by the original Chu lineage (Vol II, Vol IV).
- The Chu did not hold the Sect Head position the whole time. The founding line led for generations, then lost the seat to branch-head challenges and did not hold it for most of two centuries. The present Chu line, a cadet branch of the founders, won it back four generations ago and has held it since. "Six generations" (Vol I, Vol II) refers to this line of the family, not to six centuries. This lost-and-regained history is why the Chu take the succession so seriously.

### The Jianyun Sect (剑云宗, "Sword Cloud Sect")
- Founded over six centuries ago by the original Chu lineage, who believed the constellations should exist under one roof rather than scatter as rival schools (Vol II). Specialises in the Path of the Eight Guardian Constellations.
- Respected and ancient; not dominant. Slightly rigid; innovation tolerated only when it produces results. Reliable in contracts. Its isolation gives it independence and vulnerability (Vol I, Vol II).
- **Earns its place through contracts:** military, escort, information, security, diplomacy, instruction. Each branch takes different kinds (see the branch table).
- **Governance:** Eight semi-autonomous branches, one per constellation, each controlling its own halls, disciples, finances and internal affairs. The Sect Head rules all of them, but that authority is political as much as martial: a Sect Head who loses the branches' respect loses everything. Branch heads are simply the strongest cultivator in the branch: "no ceremony. Only the moment when everyone stops questioning it." Competition between branches is polite on the surface and ruthless underneath (Vol II).
- **Branch membership follows constellation**, not family. Chu children belong to the branch of their own constellation (Wanru in storm blue, the mother in pale jade, Meiying in Still Blade white).

## Power system

The rules here are a promise to the reader. Never break them for plot convenience.

### Core principle
All cultivators grow through the same foundation. **A Burden is a multiplier, not a requirement** (Vol I, Vol II).
- **No Burden:** steady, safe growth with a predictable ceiling.
- **With Burden:** faster growth and a higher ceiling, with a risk of collapse if the Burden breaks.
- *"Those who dare carry more... can become more."*

### The five realms
Universal for every path and constellation. The methods of climbing them differ (Vol II). What each realm concretely allows is decided by the lead writer (LW); keep it consistent.

| # | Realm | What it is | What it concretely allows (LW) | Typical lifespan (LW) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Body Tempering** | The physical foundation. Paths and constellations are discovered here. | A trained, hardened body. Qi circulates in the meridians but cannot be stored. Concentrated Qi is visible only up close (a shimmer, a glow). Forms work through body and internal circulation. | An ordinary long life, about 80 |
| 2 | **Qi Gathering** | First real contact with internal energy. Techniques begin. | A Qi reservoir forms; Qi sense at a distance; Qi can leave the skin to coat a weapon (see below). | About 100 |
| 3 | **Foundation Establishment** | The architecture of cultivation is set. | Sustained projection over tens of paces; Qi constructs; Qi armour held for a whole fight; brief running on walls or water. | About 150 |
| 4 | **Core Formation** | Qi condenses into something permanent and self-sustaining. | Qi regenerates on its own; **spiritual sense** (presence and intent, without needing Qi to read, out to a few hundred paces); presence that suppresses weaker cultivators; Qi-assisted leaps across ravines. - **Stages within a realm:** Early, Mid, Late, Peak (for example "Core Formation (Late)", "Body Tempering, Mid stage", "Qi Gathering (Peak)").
- **How stages advance (LW):** gathering Qi (meditation, tonics, marrow jade, Qi-dense places such as the cloud line and the waterfalls) **and** consolidation under pressure (real fights, hard spars, survival). Neither alone is enough, which is why a fight can trigger a stage and a quiet week can fail to.
- Breakthroughs can fail and can kill: Chu Wulong's father died mid-breakthrough (Vol II). A realm breakthrough risks **Qi deviation** if the cultivator's intent is divided (LW; see Ch 1: intent decides whether Qi holds together).

### Qi Gathering in detail (LW)
What Meiying gains at the breakthrough, and the ladder inside the realm. Built so that growth is frequent, concrete and visible.
- **The reservoir.** For the first time Qi can be stored, below the navel. Its size is measured in practice as **sequences**: how many full technique sequences a cultivator can run before the Qi thins. It grows with each stage.
- **Qi sense.** Feel other cultivators' Qi at a distance: presence, rough strength, direction of flow. Range by stage: Early about 10 paces, Mid about 25, Late about 50, Peak about 100. Dormant or deliberately suppressed Qi is harder to sense; a much higher realm can hide from a lower one. Hidden-path masking can hide from anyone.
- **Infusion and the Qi edge.** Qi can leave the skin and coat a weapon. Early: the weapon is reinforced and warm. Mid: a cutting edge of Qi a finger's width beyond the blade. Late: a hand's width. Peak: a short cut that lands a pace beyond the blade. True ranged cutting arcs belong to Foundation Establishment (or to the Bleeding Sky aspect).
- **The Qi step.** A burst of speed over a few paces from Qi driven into the legs. Short and costly at Early; longer and cheaper with each stage.
- **Toughness and healing.** Qi reinforcement lets a cultivator take Qi-enhanced blows; bruises heal in a day, cracked ribs in a couple of weeks.
- **The breakthrough itself.** Qi floods the body and spills to the surface. Warm-Qi practitioners (Meiying) feel heat and shaking. A day of weakness follows.
- **Meiying's special problem.** The Honest Blade forbids Qi embellishment, and at Qi Gathering her Qi wants to spill into every motion. Learning to hold it under the surface during Honest Blade forms (**restraint**) is a skill with its own visible steps.

### Paths vs sects (Vol I, Vol II)
- **Path:** what you are: your cultivation method, your body and soul's natural affinity. Not chosen; revealed during Body Tempering. Lineage can influence affinity but does not decide it.
- **Sect:** where you belong: an organisation, political, economic and social. A sect may specialise in a path but cannot force one on anyone. A Hollow cultivator can hide inside a Stream sect.
- Because paths are discovered, not chosen, families and sects are often disappointed or alarmed, with lasting political consequences.

### The technique hierarchy: constellation, aspect, form (Vol IV, final)
- **Constellation:** the overarching path a cultivator walks. Discovered in Body Tempering. Defines the philosophy and method.
- **Aspect:** a major domain inside a constellation, with its own philosophy of combat and cultivation. A practitioner begins with one and unlocks more as realm and mastery rise. **Aspects are tied to the constellation:** they cannot be studied from a manuscript; they must be grown into through cultivation, realm advancement and internalising what came before. The Ten Legendary Swords has ten aspects.
- **Form:** an executable technique inside an aspect. **Each aspect has seven forms.** Forms are learnable by anyone with the manuscript and the comprehension to apply it, even from another constellation. What an outsider cannot do is unlock the aspect itself.
- *"The forms are the teaching. The aspects are the understanding that makes the teaching possible in the first place."*
- **Naming convention:** Aspect — Form Number — Chinese Name — English Name. Example: **Honest Blade — First Form: Zhǐ Huā (指花) — Guiding Bloom.** In prose the author usually gives the pinyin in italics with the English after it (Ch 1, Ch 4).

### How aspects unlock (LW)
Settles the question of how Chu Linfeng reached the seventh aspect when no one has shown the Honest Blade's seventh form.
- **Three requirements, all needed:**
  1. **The realm gate** for that aspect (table below).
  2. **The previous aspect internalised:** its first form **Mastered** and **three other forms at Reliable or better**. The seventh form is **not** required; it is a summit almost no one reaches.
  3. **A moment of comprehension** that cannot be forced or bought. It comes from pressure, insight, or someone else's piece of the answer.
- **Realm gates for the Ten Legendary Swords:**

| Aspect | Earliest realm |
|---|---|
| 1. Honest Blade | On awakening (Body Tempering) |
| 2. Whispering Edge | Qi Gathering |

  (Shen Qiuyue, Foundation Establishment Mid, has three aspects. Chu Linfeng reached the seventh at Core Formation Peak.)
- **The glimpse.** A practitioner who has met the realm gate and stands close to the other requirements can, under extreme pressure, **touch the next aspect once**: a single, involuntary expression of it. It cannot be repeated at will, it costs heavily (Qi exhaustion, a strained channel), and it does not count as unlocking. It tells the practitioner, and the reader, what is coming.

### The Honest Blade (诚刃, Chéng Rèn), First Aspect (Vol III)
*"The blade that goes where it needs to go. Nothing added. Nothing withheld."*
*"It does not matter how hard you strike if you are striking in the wrong place. The Honest Blade finds the right place first."*

- **Philosophy:** respond only to what is real. Most cultivators fight the opponent they have decided they face; the Honest Blade abandons that decision. Every later aspect assumes this one as its ground.
- **Weapon:** one-handed jian, chosen for precision and speed over power. **All seven forms work unarmed** with no change in principle (Meiying used Guiding Bloom bare-handed in Ch 1).
- **Qi use:** minimal to none in early forms. Raw Qi enhancement is actively discouraged because it interferes with precise reading.
- **Signature:** reads the opponent's body, Qi flow and committed motion to find openings that already exist.
- **Weakness:** loses its advantage against opponents who are truly unpredictable or have no readable pattern (shown by Wanru in Ch 4).
- **Guard motion:** Meiying's hand runs the Honest Blade guard position when she is thinking (Ch 1).

### Qi basics shown on the page (Ch 1)
- **Offensive Qi** moves forward and compresses. **Defensive Qi** spreads and anchors. **Projection Qi** releases.
- "Qi does not have a preference. It moves where your intent directs it." Strength decides how much Qi is released; **intent** decides whether it holds together over distance or disperses on impact.
- Basic exercise: circulate through the primary meridians in a full cycle, compressing on the forward pass and releasing on the return, and hold it for ten seconds.
- Concentrated Qi can be seen with ordinary eyes: Elder Fang's circulation shows as a faint shimmer like heat over stone; Wen Jiahao's fully infused fists glow faint orange and raise the temperature noticeably (Ch 1).
- A disrupted Qi circuit leaves the hands dark and trembling for a while as it reassembles (Ch 1).
- "Qi is not a tool. It's more like a conversation" (Wanru, Ch 4).

### Unintended channelling and Meiying's secondary channel (Ch 4, Ch 5, Notes 4)
- **Unintended channelling:** described in a theoretical text on meridian behaviour in high-affinity practitioners. When Qi meets resistance in a primary route, it can spontaneously find a secondary path. The text treats it as involuntary.
- **Meiying's discovery:** the third meridian resistance was not an obstruction. It was her Qi pointing to a secondary channel that has existed since she was twelve. By listening instead of pushing, she can open it on purpose.
- **Geometry:** it curves along the south face of the third meridian cluster and reaches the same junction as the forward pass, faster and without resistance.
- **Gains:** cleaner, faster circulation; Qi arrives already fitted to the technique; Parting Reed's telegraph is smaller; Rooted Shore's threshold is higher; her approach to breakthrough is noticeably shorter. The channel will widen with use.
- **Limits (Ch 5):** under sustained output it narrows after about **three** full technique sequences, where the forward pass lasts five or six (more efficient, less durable). There is a half-breath **initiation delay** of listening before it opens; the delay is shrinking day by day.
- **How it grows (LW):** use widens it, and so does learning to listen *between* sequences (a rhythm of letting it rest for a breath mid-fight). Its endurance is one of her trackable numbers. When it matches the forward pass, stop tracking it separately.

### Meiying's perception (LW)
- **What everyone gets:** at Qi Gathering, every cultivator gains Qi sense (presence, strength, direction of flow; ranges above).
- **What only she has:** she reads how Qi is **built**: where a technique is being assembled, the joints and seams in it, where an imposed structure (a domain, a seal) ends, and where a Burden's condition sits inside a person. Other cultivators feel how much Qi and which way it moves. She feels its architecture.
- **Where it comes from:** the Honest Blade's discipline of responding only to what is real, joined to her constellation. It is the root of what the tenth aspect will be, and the reason the tenth aspect fits her name. The secondary channel is a sign of her high affinity, not the cause.
- **Steps (each is a visible milestone):**
  1. Bodies, habits and visible Qi (now; Ch 1 to 6).
  2. The **edge** of an imposed Qi structure, such as the sealed hearing (Arc 1, after Qi Gathering).
  3. The **weight** of a Burden carried by another person (Arc 1).
  4. The **shape** of a condition: a rough sense of what someone's Burden is bound to (Arc 2). Politically explosive, since Burdens are secret.
  5. Later steps are tied to the aspects and to the tenth.
- **Limits:** it needs her attention and stillness (the Honest Blade's reading state); crowds of Qi blur it; it cannot read Qi that announces nothing (Hidden-path masking, such as Shen Wuque's). At first she thinks it is just being observant, and so does everyone else.

### The Burden system (Vol I, Vol II, Ch 2, Ch 5)
*"Burdens are not chains. They are contracts. And like all contracts, they must be read carefully before signing."*

- **Definition:** a self-imposed or accepted condition that binds a cultivator's power to something external or internal. It sits on top of normal cultivation; it amplifies it and does not replace it. "A Burden does not make you stronger. It makes the strength you already have more expensive to maintain, and more valuable because of it" (Shen Cailan, Ch 2).
- **Three types:**
  - **External:** bound to something outside the self: protect a person, guard a place or object, serve a sect, cause or ideal. Easier to gain, harder to keep. Because the binding depends on things the cultivator cannot control (people die, places are destroyed), External Burdens have **the highest rate of catastrophic failure** (Suyin, confirmed by Shen Cailan, Ch 2). A **custodial** Burden (keeping a thing safe or in a place) is a kind of External Burden (LW).
  - **Internal:** bound against the self: limit your own output, seal part of your Qi, restrict techniques or weapons. Slower early growth, massive late payoff. Harder to acquire because of the discipline required.
  - **Existential:** bound to fate: carry a curse willingly, bind your cultivation to an artifact, share fate with another person (their death affects your cultivation directly). Highest risk, highest reward. Rare.
- **Three inseparable parts:**
  - **Amplification:** faster Qi absorption, stronger techniques, higher breakthrough success. Scales with severity.
  - **Condition:** a rule that must be maintained, defined by the Burden. **Cannot be renegotiated after acceptance.**
  - **Penalty:** backlash if the condition breaks.
- **Example, Oath of Protection:** External; one person; +20% Qi recovery speed; +10% reaction speed when the target is near; condition: actively protect the target from lethal harm; penalty if the target dies: higher Qi deviation risk and temporary cultivation instability.
- **Backlash:**
  - The **break** of the condition is what triggers backlash, not the Burden's age (Notes 5). Backlash strikes **at the moment of the break**, wherever the cultivator is (LW).
  - **Severity** scales with the Burden's level, how long it was carried (Vol II) and how deeply it is integrated (Ch 2).
  - Tiers (Notes 5): a minor Burden breaking means Qi instability and temporary disruption. A significant one means **Qi deviation**: circuit damage, lost progress, long recovery. A load-bearing one can cause **Qi implosion**: the whole circuit collapses inward, fatal at most cultivation levels (Elder Fang).
  - **At Core Formation or above** (the answer Shen Cailan withheld; LW): a catastrophic break can **sever the cultivator from their constellation**. They keep their realm and lose every aspect beyond the first. Revealed in Arc 2.
  - "Cultivators have died from broken Burdens. Not many. Enough" (Ch 2).
- **Timing:** Burdens taken before Foundation Establishment integrate more deeply into the cultivation base, with higher amplification and higher risk. Burdens taken later are more stable but less transformative (Ch 2).
- **Fit:** a Burden interacts with path and constellation. A warm-Qi practitioner taking an Internal Burden that restricts Qi flow must account for the compounding effect. "The condition has to fit the cultivator, not just what they want, but what they actually are" (Ch 2). Family philosophy shapes how a cultivator approaches Burdens; neither "strength is purely internal" nor the opposite is a complete picture (Ch 2).
- **Records:** the Iron Sash keeps records of active Burdens among senior members (Ch 5). Some entries are sealed so that only the Iron Sash head may read them (LW).
- **Shared Burdens and seal-craft (LW):** two or more cultivators can accept **one** Burden together. The condition binds them all and the penalty falls on them all. The Qiongling houses are masters of this; western sects barely know it is possible. It is how the sealed hearing works.
- **The five rules (author's rules for the story):**
  1. Burdens are optional; no cultivator is required to carry one.
  2. More Burden is not always better; each must be chosen strategically.
  3. Breaking a Burden has real and lasting consequences.
  4. Most cultivators actively avoid them.
  5. **The protagonist is not the only one who understands them, just better at it than most.**
- **How the world sees them:** most cultivators see Burdens as chains, weakness, traps for the desperate. The strongest cultivators carry several, hide them and master them. The gap between belief and elite practice is one of the great unspoken secrets of the cultivation world.

### The sealed hearing (the silence domain; Ch 3, Notes 3; explained LW)
- **On the page:** a complete, edged absence of sound around the hollow meeting. Speech, breath and footsteps did not carry; the birds on that slope were silent; the mist and wind moved normally. Body Tempering students lack the cultivation and theory to identify it (Ch 3).
- **What it is (Hidden):** a **shared Burden** accepted by everyone speaking inside it, made with Qiongling seal-craft. Condition: **no one outside its edge may hear a word spoken inside.** Amplification: the silence itself; sound cannot cross outward, while light, mist and wind pass normally. Penalty: if the condition breaks, **every speaker inside** suffers load-bearing backlash (implosion).
- **How it can break:** ordinary sound cannot cross. But a cultivator can force a seam in the edge with a Qi strike, or thread a listening technique through it (the Iron Sash's Binding Sash cultivators can). If words are heard through the seam, the speakers die. This is why the Iron Sash has never listened in, and why a listening post would be a weapon.
- **The edge** can be felt by Meiying's perception once she reaches Qi Gathering.

### Talent
- Constellation is discovered, not chosen; lineage influences but does not decide it.
- Affinity matters (high-affinity practitioners can show unintended channelling).
- The chapters stress **effort and comprehension** over "genius": Meiying's results come from hours of work and from understanding (Ch 1, Ch 4). The Honest Blade's forms are locked behind comprehension, not bloodline (Vol IV).

### Rare, forbidden or hidden powers

## Style notes (taken from the author's chapters)

- **Chapter format:**
  - `# Chapter N: Title`
  - An italic epigraph of short broken lines, then `—`, then an italic attribution (an inscription, a saying, or an instructor's words).
  - Scene breaks are `―――`.
  - The chapter closes with `*— End of Chapter N —*` (the author writes it as "End of Chapter One", "End of Chapter Two" and so on).
- **Voice:** Close, observant, analytical. Meiying notices concrete details (a short sleeve, a brush grip, a shoulder drop) and the prose follows her mind as she assembles patterns. Long, rhythmic sensory passages for places (the outer sect, the slopes), then short paragraphs and one-line beats for impact ("He had seen everything.").
- **Dialogue:** Quick and clipped among the trio; characters dodge and imply. Suyin's silences carry meaning, and the narration translates them ("Which with Suyin meant: think about it").
- **Fights:** Grounded and readable. Spatial layout, cause and effect, and Meiying's reading of the opponent are visible throughout. Wins are narrow and earned ("Barely."). She takes hits and bruises.
- **Terminology:** Qi (capital Q), meridians, jian, dao, Body Tempering and the other realm names in English, technique names in pinyin with characters in reference material and English glosses. Branch names in English (Iron Sash, Still Blade).
- **Address:** "Elder Fang", "my lady" from staff. Surname first (Chu Meiying, Han Jingwei). Friends use given names.
- **Humour:** From character: Meiying's deadpan verdicts, Chengqi's enthusiasm, Suyin's one-line precision. Never undercuts the stakes.
- **The "genius" rule:** whenever someone calls her a genius, she goes quiet. Only her father may say it.
- **Recurring motifs:** plum blossoms; filing things away; honesty and the blade; weight and carrying (Burdens); silence; reading people.
- **For co-writers, keep an eye on these (they are the author's own habits, so trim lightly rather than remove):** "filed it away" / "filed this" recurs in almost every chapter; "the particular / specific quality of" recurs often; em dashes are frequent. Vary these in new chapters so they do not become a tic readers notice.
