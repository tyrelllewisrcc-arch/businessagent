## Premise

### The author's premise **[Author canon]**

> Chu Meiying is a young disciple of the Jianyun Sect whose unusually sharp perception draws her into a mystery hidden beneath ordinary sect life. While training, cultivating, and spending time with her friends Suyin and Luo Chengqi, the three accidentally witness a secret meeting between a Jianyun elder and a mysterious outsider, something they were never meant to see.
>
> Their discovery coincides with increasingly suspicious events inside the sect, including Elder Fang's mysterious death, a sealed letter, an unknown geometric emblem, and indications that members of the sect may be involved in something much larger.
>
> As Meiying and her friends quietly investigate, they risk attracting the attention of people far more powerful than themselves. At the same time, Meiying is progressing through her own cultivation journey, discovering that her ability to perceive and understand Qi may be far more unusual than she initially realizes.
>
> At its core: a cultivation mystery/adventure about three young disciples uncovering a conspiracy within their sect while the heroine gradually grows from an ordinary disciple into a powerful cultivator.

### Expanded premise **[Proposal — author to confirm]**

Chu Meiying has been an outer disciple of the Jianyun Sect for three years and is still stuck at the third layer of Qi Gathering. The sect's rule is simple: reach the fifth layer by your third Winter Assessment or be sent back down the mountain. Her assessment is thirty-seven days away. Her family in the town below depends on the silver she sends home.

What nobody knows is why she is stuck. Meiying sees qi. Not the vague "sense" every cultivator has, but the actual currents: the lines a formation channels, the path qi takes through a body, and the faint shadows it leaves behind after it has moved. The sect's standard cultivation manual tells her to move her qi one way; her eyes tell her the qi wants to go another. Torn between the two, she has gone nowhere.

One night, sneaking into the forbidden Old Plum Grove with her friends Suyin and Luo Chengqi, Meiying spots a seam in a hidden concealment formation and the three of them overhear a hooded Jianyun elder talking to a veiled outsider who carries a bronze token marked with a strange geometric emblem. The conversation sounds like treason. Two days later Elder Fang, the gruff formation master who had just noticed Meiying's odd talent, is found dead in seclusion. The sect calls it qi deviation. Meiying's eyes see murder.

The case is the engine of Arc 1. Under it lies a bigger truth: the world's qi is slowly running out (the Thinning), the great sects are hiding it, and a secret society called the Measure is willing to drain whole mountains to keep the road to Heaven open for a chosen few. Meiying's gift is exactly what both sides of that war need, and the further she climbs, the harder it becomes to stay hidden.

**Theme:** Seeing is not the same as understanding. Meiying's eyes never lie, but she misreads what they show her, and she has to learn to trust people as well as her own perception.

- **Biggest flaw:** **[Proposal — author to confirm]** Certainty. She trusts her eyes over people, keeps secrets by reflex, and when she has seen something she stops questioning her interpretation of it. In Arc 1 this makes her misread the meeting completely.

  - Movement: Drifting Cloud Step (basic footwork).
  - Her unnamed perception (see Power system: Qi Sight).
- **Way of speaking:** **[Proposal — author to confirm]** Precise and economical. Asks questions instead of making statements. Dry, understated humour. Borrows her father's carpenter sayings ("Measure twice, cut once." "A joint that needs glue was cut wrong."). Goes very quiet, not loud, when angry. Does not say what she feels; she does something about it.

## World **[Proposal — author to confirm, except where tagged]**

- **Setting overview:** The Eastern Reach of a large continent. The mortal Kingdom of Huai rules the lowlands and leaves the cultivation sects alone in exchange for their help against beast tides from the Wild Reaches in the north. Sects are built on spirit veins: underground rivers of qi that gather under certain mountains. A sect is only as strong as its vein.
- **The Jianyun Sect:** **[Author canon: the sect's name]** A proposed meaning is 剑云, "Sword-Cloud". A mid-tier orthodox sword sect founded about 900 years ago. Roughly 300 outer disciples, 80 inner disciples, twelve elders, one Sect Master and some 500 mortal servants. Famous for its sword arts and for the Cloud Veil.
- **The Cloud Veil:** The Mountain-Guarding Array. It produces the permanent sea of cloud that rings Jianyun Mountain at mid-height and hides and protects the sect. It runs on the vein through **nine nodes** around the mountain. Old disciples say the cloud sea used to sit higher; it drops a little every year. (A Thinning clue.)
- **Key locations:**
  - **Cloudpierce Peak:** the summit. The Sect Master's hall and the Founders' Hall.
  - **Sword Cliff:** the main training ground, a broad stone shelf above the cloud sea.
  - **Outer Court:** the lower terraces, with the Pine Dormitories (outer-disciple rooms), dining hall, and the Steward's Office and supply stores.
  - **Formation Hall:** on the east shoulder among bronze array pillars. Fang's seclusion chamber is cut into the rock behind it.
  - **Medicine Hall:** in the south valley, with terraced herb gardens.
  - **Discipline Hall:** black-tiled, beside the main gate.
  - **Scripture Pavilion:** the library, open to outer disciples only on the lower floor.
  - **North Slope and the Old Plum Grove:** forbidden to disciples ("unstable ground"). Ancient plum trees grow there, along with rare frost-dew orchids. The grove sits over the Cloud Veil's **Seventh Node**, which is why qi pools there and why the plums bloom so strongly.
  - **Yunxia Town:** the mortal town at the foot of the mountain, by the ferry. Meiying's family lives there.
  - **Heavenly Ledger Pavilion:** an allied scholarly sect about 200 li east. Astronomers, archivists, formation theorists.
- **Factions:**
  - **The Jianyun Sect.** **[Author canon]**
  - **The Measure:** a secret society of formation masters and high cultivators (see Secrets). Its members call themselves Surveyors.
  - **The Heavenly Ledger Pavilion:** an allied sect, unknowingly hosting a Surveyor.
  - **Other sects:** left vague for now. There are about nine major vein-sects in the Eastern Reach; this number matters later.
- **Technology and magic level:** Pre-industrial, like late imperial China: paper, ink, bronze, silk, ferries, no gunpowder weapons. Cultivators fly briefly on swords only from Foundation Building upward. Formations, talismans and pills are the main "magic technology".
- **Calendar and time:** Months are numbered (Eleventh Month, Twelfth Month). Chapter 1 opens on the 3rd day of the Eleventh Month in early winter. Short durations can be given as "an incense stick's time" (about half an hour), but use plain language most of the time.
- **Currency:** Spirit stones (low, middle and high grade) for cultivators. Contribution points inside the sect (earned through chores and missions, spent at the stores and the Scripture Pavilion). Silver taels for mortals. Outer disciples receive 2 low-grade spirit stones and 3 silver taels a month; Meiying sends her silver home.
- **Isekai rules:** Not an isekai. Not used.

## Power system **[Proposal — author to confirm, except where tagged]**

The rules here are a promise to the reader. Never break them for plot convenience. **[Author canon: Meiying progresses from an ordinary disciple to a powerful cultivator, and her ability to perceive and understand Qi is far more unusual than she first realises.]**

- **Name of the system:** Cultivation. Drawing the world's qi into the body's meridians and refining it in the dantian (the energy centre below the navel).

### Realms (lowest to highest)

| Realm | Stages | What it means | Typical lifespan | Who's here at Jianyun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Qi Gathering** | Layers 1 to 9 | Draw ambient qi through breathing methods, open meridians, fill the dantian. | ~100 | Outer disciples |
| **Foundation Building** | Early, Middle, Late, Peak | Compress gathered qi into a dense liquid "foundation". Spiritual sense awakens (a few paces, growing to about 20). Brief sword flight. | ~150 | Inner disciples, deacons, stewards |
| **Core Formation** | Early, Middle, Late, Peak | Condense the foundation into a solid core. Spiritual sense covers a hall, later a valley. | ~300 | Elders |
| **Nascent Soul** | Early, Middle, Late, Peak | The core births a soul-infant; the soul can briefly survive the body's death. Spiritual sense covers a mountain. | ~800 | The Sect Master |
| **Soul Transformation** | Not known to common disciples | The soul reshapes the body. A handful exist in the whole Eastern Reach. | ~2,000 | None |
| **Ascension** | Not known | Leaving the mortal world for the Heavens. No confirmed ascension in about 1,100 years (a Thinning clue). | n/a | Legend |

- **Qi Gathering milestones:**
  - Layers 1 to 3: sensing and drawing qi; slightly stronger, faster body.
  - Layers 4 to 6: qi can be pushed into limbs and weapons. **Layer 5 is the sect's minimum**: an outer disciple who hasn't reached it by their third Winter Assessment is sent down the mountain.
  - Layers 7 to 9: short sword-qi projection (a few inches past the blade), light-body footwork. Layer 7 and above may attempt the inner-disciple trial.
  - Peak of Layer 9: may attempt Foundation Building.
- **Typical pace:** A talented disciple reaches Layer 5 within a year; an average one in two to three years. Meiying is at Layer 3 after three years, well behind.

### How progress is gained
- **Accumulation:** daily cultivation with a method (manual), ideally on or near a spirit vein. Spirit stones and pills speed it up.
- **Insight:** each breakthrough also needs understanding: a correct grasp of how qi should flow at the next level. Raw qi without insight just piles up and causes blockages. (This is why Meiying is stuck: she has the accumulated qi but her insight and her manual contradict each other.)
- **Resources:** pills (alchemy), spirit stones, vein-rich locations, and teachers.
- **Risk:** breakthroughs can fail. Foundation Building fails about half the time on a first attempt.

### Costs, limits and risks
- **Qi deviation:** qi flowing against the meridians' structure, forced too fast, or disturbed during a breakthrough. Mild cases burn the meridians and set cultivation back. Severe cases reverse the flow and kill. Most likely during breakthroughs and closed-door seclusion. **Deviations have been rising for decades** (a Thinning clue), and this is what makes "Fang died of qi deviation" believable.
- **Spiritual sense** cannot pass through a concealment formation in either direction. Those inside a concealment array are also blind to the outside. This is why the people at the secret meeting didn't sense the trio.
- **Spirit veins are finite.** (A secret: they are thinning everywhere.)
- **Breakthroughs show physically:** a rush of heat or cold, the dantian expanding, impurities sweating out (black, foul-smelling sweat at the early Qi Gathering layers), sharper senses afterwards.

### What makes someone talented or untalented
- **Spiritual root:** element affinities (metal, wood, water, fire, earth, plus rarer variants) and purity (graded upper, upper-middle, lower-middle, lower). Purity sets the speed of accumulation.
- **Comprehension:** how quickly someone gains insight. This matters more than most sects admit.
- **Meiying** has an ordinary root and extraordinary perception, which can feed comprehension once she learns to use it.

### Formations (arrays)
- Patterns of lines, stones, inscriptions and anchors (sometimes living things, such as trees) that channel qi into an effect: protection, concealment, gathering, sealing, killing.
- **Geometry is everything.** A single misplaced or missing line changes or breaks the effect.
- **Seams:** every formation has joints where its lines meet. A well-made seam is invisible to spiritual sense. A disturbance at an anchor makes the whole array ripple.
- **Formation locks:** a sealed object (such as Fang's letter) can be locked with a formation that destroys it if opened wrongly.
- **Smoke-lines:** a line can be drawn in a formation temporarily with inscribed incense. The smoke carries the line; when it disperses the line is gone and only a "scar" remains. (This is how Fang was killed.)

### Qi Sight: Meiying's gift **[Author canon: her perception of Qi is unusual, and more unusual than she realises]**
Everything in this subsection is a **[Proposal — author to confirm]**.

- **What it is (as she understands it at the start):** She sees qi as moving light with texture, and she can "feel" a person's qi signature (a texture, a temperature, a quirk). She thinks most cultivators see "a bit" of this. She has no name for it.
- **What it actually is (revealed gradually):** A separate faculty from spiritual sense. Spiritual sense detects presence and quantity of qi; Meiying's sight perceives **structure**: the paths qi travels, the lines of formations, and the marks qi leaves after it moves. Old texts call this gift the **Plum Blossom Shadow** (梅影), from a saying by an ancient formation master: *"Qi passes like the shadow of a plum branch on snow: there for a moment, and true in every line."* People born with it were the founders of formation study. The Measure has searched for one for centuries.
- **What she can do at the start:**
  - See active qi currents and strong formation lines faintly (enough to spot a missing line in a practice array).
  - Read people's qi signatures by texture.
  - See her own qi move, which is why the standard manual feels "wrong" to her.
- **What she learns in Arc 1:**
  - **Reading qi shadows:** the residue qi leaves behind after use. From a shadow she can tell roughly what technique was used, its texture (and therefore often who used it), its direction, and how long ago.
  - **Fading rule:** a casual technique leaves a shadow lasting minutes to an hour; a strong Foundation Building technique, a day or so; a Core Formation killing technique, around a week, fading all the while.
  - **Imprints:** an object someone has carried or used for years (a pipe, a sword, a brush) holds a faint lasting imprint of their signature. Imprints last months to years.
  - **Scars:** where a formation line existed and was removed or dispersed, a scar remains for weeks.
  - **Inscriptions:** an active inscription (on a talisman, array stone or incense stick) carries the inscriber's signature for as long as the inscription lasts, because it is live qi, not a shadow.
  - **Seeing seams and flaws** in formations, including concealment arrays that block spiritual sense.
- **Costs and limits (never break these):**
  - It needs focus. While reading shadows she is slow, distracted and vulnerable.
  - Strain causes headaches, then nosebleeds, then **whiteout**: her qi sight and sometimes ordinary vision go white for minutes to days.
  - Pushing past whiteout risks permanent damage.
  - She cannot read thoughts or intentions, only qi.
  - Perfectly folded qi (see below) looks like a blank: an absence, not a signature.
  - Sealed formation locks resist her; she can study them but opening one takes weeks of work and growth.
  - Shadows fade. A clue missed is gone.
  - Later arcs: shadows can be faked by someone who knows how she sees. She can be fooled.
- **Growth path (rough):** Arc 1, shadows, seams and flaws. Arc 2, seeing the structure of living meridians (reading and countering techniques as they form, diagnosing). Arc 3+, perceiving the Heavenly Lattice, the underlying structure through which all qi flows. Endgame, touching the lattice: re-weaving formations and, ultimately, the world's veins.
- **Its effect on her cultivation:** Once she learns to follow the flow she sees rather than the manual's diagram ("Follow the flow the diagram is trying to describe" — Fang's margin note), her years of piled-up qi start to settle. This explains a fast early climb without breaking the rules: the qi was already there, and what she lacked was the insight to use it.

### Rare or forbidden powers
- **The Folded Meridian Art (the Measure's secret method):** folds the cultivator's qi on itself like creased paper. It hides one's true realm (a Core Formation Peak can appear as Foundation Building), lets one slip through formation seams, and at full fold makes the user's qi look blank to Meiying's sight. It leaves a distinctive **creased** texture in shadows and on things the user handles. Pei practises a crude version, so his fingertips carry faint creases.
- **The Measure's silencing restriction:** a seal placed in a member's meridians. If they begin to reveal certain names, it triggers, burns the emblem into their chest and kills them.
- **Heart-demon oaths:** a binding oath sworn on one's Dao heart. Breaking it causes heart demons during breakthroughs. Used by sects for secrecy.

## Style notes **[Proposal — author to confirm]**

- **Voice:** Clean, grounded, concrete prose. Epic in scale but intimate in focus: frost on a plum branch, the smell of incense ash, the click of a jade token. Let grandeur come from the mountain, the cloud sea and the stakes, not from purple prose.
- **Terminology:** Use *qi*, *dantian*, *meridians*. Translate everything else into English (Qi Gathering, Cloud Veil, Formation Hall). Keep the glossary small; add new terms only when Meiying needs them.
- **Names:** Surname first (Chu Meiying, Luo Chengqi). Address forms: "Senior Brother", "Senior Sister", "Junior Sister", "Elder Fang", "Steward Pei". Friends use given names.
- **Qi descriptions:** Always use the signature table above. These are clues, so consistency matters.
- **Fair-play rule:** Every clue Meiying perceives must appear on the page when she perceives it, even if she doesn't understand it. Never hide a clue from the reader that Meiying has.
- **Recurring motifs:** missing lines; folding (paper cranes, folded qi); measuring ("Measure twice, cut once" against "the Measure"); plum blossoms in snow; her father's ink-line reel.
- **Humour:** Comes from character. Fang's insults, Suyin's commentary, Chengqi's deadpan. No modern slang, no fourth-wall jokes.
- **Fights:** Grounded. Outer disciples fight like trained teenagers, not gods. Meiying wins with perception, timing and cleverness, not raw power. Blood when it happens; no lingering gore.
- **Avoid:** face-slapping scenes, "courting death!", "this young master", calling people "ants", "jade beauty" descriptions, and characters announcing their feelings.
- **Breakthrough scenes:** Build up first (the struggle, the risk), then give the moment weight: a new sensation, something physical, and people noticing.
